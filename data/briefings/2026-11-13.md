# Weekly briefing: week to Friday 13 November 2026

## In brief

- World stocks rose 4.4% in pounds (2.4% in US dollars). The pound fell 1.8% against the dollar, which lifts overseas shares for a UK investor.
- Japan had a strong week: Japanese banks rose 10.0% in pounds, and five Japanese sectors moved up into the second band.
- Gold miners and Canadian materials fell between 4% and 6% in pounds, and their scores dropped by 14 to 21 each, all to 26.
- Taiwan had the best week of any country (up 11.2% in pounds) and the biggest score rise (up 15, to 72). Spain moved into the top band.
- Both top-scored practice portfolios are behind world stocks and behind their lowest-scored rivals. After about six weeks, this means very little.

## The big picture

World stocks (MSCI ACWI) rose 4.4% in pounds and 2.4% in dollars this week, and are up 10.9% in pounds over the year. The US market (S&P 500) rose 4.5% in pounds and 2.6% in dollars.

Our economic read is **mid cycle**: steady growth. The 10-year US interest rate (5.15%) is 1.03% above the 3-month rate (4.12%), a normal gap. Copper, which follows factory and building demand, has gained 6.2% on gold over 3 months, pointing to stronger growth, while oil is down 16% over 6 months, which cuts business costs but often signals weaker demand. The fear gauge (VIX, which measures how much investors expect prices to swing) is a normal 19.3. In this phase, technology, communication services, industrials and financials usually do well.

For countries, two conditions matter: the US dollar has risen 3.8% against major currencies over 3 months, and chip-maker shares did 14.5% worse than world stocks over 3 months, a drag on chip-heavy markets.

## Sectors

Each sector is compared with the whole stock market of its own region; for example, US health care is compared with the S&P 500. Scores run from 0 to 100 in five bands, from "Avoid" at the bottom to the top band.

**On top.** US health care (90, compared with the S&P 500) leads, followed by Japanese financials other than banks (88, compared with the TOPIX), global health care (86, compared with world stocks), European chemicals (86, compared with the STOXX Europe 600) and US energy (84, compared with the S&P 500). US energy rose 7.4% in pounds (5.4% in dollars, ahead of the S&P 500's 2.6%).

**At the bottom.** Uranium and nuclear (1), solar (3), China internet (4), robotics and AI (8) and clean energy (9), all compared with the S&P 500, are in "Avoid" and in a downtrend. Solar and China internet had the worst weeks of any sector, down 6.9% and 6.1% in pounds.

**Japan moving up.** Japanese sectors, compared with the TOPIX, took all five of the week's best places, led by construction and materials (up 10.5% in pounds) and banks (up 10.0%). Electric power and gas rose 20 points, to 62, and raw materials and chemicals 15, to 63. Both moved into the second band, as did trading companies, pharmaceuticals and retail.

**Gold miners down.** The Canadian gold-miner fund fell 5.8% in pounds and its score fell 21, to 26; Canadian materials fell 4.4% and lost 14, also to 26. Both are compared with the S&P/TSX 60 and dropped from the middle band to the fourth. The global gold-miner fund (compared with the S&P 500) fell 5.1% and lost 14, to 26. Gold-miner shares tend to follow the gold price, with bigger swings.

**Industrials recovering.** US, global and European industrials all moved up from the fourth band to the middle one; US industrials rose 5.7% in pounds. Cloud computing (63) moved into the second band, while lithium and batteries (15) dropped into "Avoid".

## Countries

Each country's main stock index is compared with world stocks (MSCI ACWI), with both measured in the same currency.

**Top and bottom.** Poland (91), Spain (83), the UK (79), Canada (78) and Switzerland (78) score highest. Spain rose 9 and moved into the top band after a 5.3% rise in pounds; Canada fell 6 and dropped out of it. The lowest scores are Indonesia (1), Argentina (8), China (11), Turkey (17) and the Philippines (19), all in "Avoid" and in a downtrend.

**Taiwan up, Hong Kong down.** Taiwan rose 11.2% in pounds, well ahead of world stocks' 4.4%, and moved into the second band. It is up 55.1% over the year, though both of today's conditions count against it. South Korea rose 6.2% and Japan 5.9%. The Hang Seng fell 3.4% in Hong Kong dollars and Hong Kong's score fell 14, to 47, into the middle band. Germany rose 10, to 52, and Italy moved into the second band (60).

**Weakest weeks.** China fell 2.7% in pounds, the Philippines 2.2% and South Africa 1.9%, all well behind world stocks.

**Currencies.** Against the dollar, the Brazilian real fell 2.9%, the South African rand 2.3% and the Swiss franc 2.2%; the Korean won rose 2.0%. South Africa's market fell only 0.3% in rand, but the weaker rand made it a 1.9% loss in pounds.

**Cheap and holding up.** Mexico, Brazil, Sweden, Norway and Poland look cheap compared with other markets while keeping middling or better scores.

## Practice portfolios

The practice portfolios started on Tuesday 29 September with a pretend £10,000 each, one holding the top-scored 20% and one the lowest-scored 20%.

- **Sectors:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £9,630, behind the lowest-scored (£10,178), the picks' own markets (£9,923) and world stocks (£9,807). Among the current top picks, US consumer staples and European insurance have done best, Japanese foods worst.
- **Countries:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £9,208 and the lowest-scored £9,577, both behind world stocks (£9,807). Poland has been the best of the current top picks.

About six weeks of results mean very little. On past data, the top-scored 20% of sectors did 5.5% a year better than the lowest-scored 20%, with a reliability measure (t) of 1.2, where 2 or more is convincing. For countries the test gave −1.4% a year (t = −0.3): the score has not reliably picked winning countries.
