# Weekly briefing: week to Friday 27 November 2026

## In brief

- A calmer week: world stocks rose 2.3% in pounds (1.9% in US dollars) and the US market rose 2.1% in pounds (1.7% in dollars).
- The fear gauge (VIX) is at 23.8, inside its normal range of 16 to 25. Our economic read is **mid-to-contraction**, and the US market is still in a broad downtrend.
- Gold miners and biotech had the best weeks. Canadian gold miners rose 14.7% in pounds, yet their score stayed in "Avoid".
- Brazil rose 12.6% in pounds, about half of it from a 6.8% rise in its currency. Its score still fell.
- The top-scored sectors and countries barely changed: Japanese financials other than banks (88) and Poland (88) lead.

## The big picture

World stocks (MSCI ACWI) rose 2.3% in pounds and 1.9% in dollars this week. They are up 9.6% in pounds over the year. The US market (S&P 500) rose 2.1% in pounds and 1.7% in dollars.

Our economic read is **mid-to-contraction**, meaning the signs are mixed. On the cautious side, the S&P 500 is below its 200-day (about 10-month) average price, a broad downtrend. The gap between the 10-year US interest rate (4.94%) and the 3-month rate (4.15%) has shrunk by 0.21% in 3 months, which often happens late in an expansion. On the brighter side, copper has gained 5.4% on gold over 3 months, which points to stronger growth, and the fear gauge (VIX, which measures how much investors expect prices to swing) is at 23.8, in its normal range.

For countries, four conditions matter today: the US dollar has risen 4.3% against major currencies over 3 months; copper is down 15% over 3 months, bad for mining-heavy markets; investors are still nervous, with world stocks 1.8% below their 200-day average; and chip-maker shares did 13.9% worse than world stocks over 3 months.

## Sectors

Each sector is compared with the whole stock market of its own region; for example, US health care is compared with the S&P 500. Scores run from 0 to 100 in five bands, from "Avoid" at the bottom to the top band.

**On top.** Japanese financials other than banks (88, compared with the TOPIX) lead, followed by US health care (86, compared with the S&P 500), European insurance (85) and European chemicals (84), both compared with the STOXX Europe 600, and US energy (83). All five were already in the top band.

**At the bottom.** Uranium and nuclear (2), solar (3), China internet (6), clean energy (9) and lithium and batteries (12), all compared with the S&P 500, are in "Avoid" and in a downtrend.

**Gold miners and biotech.** The best weeks in pounds were Canadian gold miners (up 14.7%) and Canadian materials (up 14.3%), both compared with the S&P/TSX 60. Both stay in "Avoid" (19 and 18): one good week does not change a score built on many months. Gold miners tend to follow the gold price. Biotech rose 10.4% in pounds and its score rose 11, to 80 (compared with the S&P 500). US homebuilders rose 9.1%.

**US materials move up.** US materials gained 5.9% in pounds (5.5% in dollars, against the S&P 500's 1.7%). The score rose 11, to 70, into the second band. US consumer discretionary (goods and services people can put off buying) rose 7, to 44, into the middle band.

**Scores slipping.** Canadian technology (compared with the S&P/TSX 60) fell 9, to 31, and dropped to the fourth band, even though it rose 6.0% in pounds. US regional banks (compared with the S&P 500) fell 8, to 57, and European banks moved to the middle band (56). European basic resources (35) and industrial goods and services (38) dropped to the fourth band, compared with the STOXX Europe 600.

**Japan.** The worst week was Japanese IT and services, down 3.3% in pounds (3.7% in yen); it dropped to the middle band (56, compared with the TOPIX). Steel and nonferrous metals fell 2.3% in pounds. Trading companies, foods, and raw materials and chemicals each moved into the second band at 62.

## Countries

Each country's main stock index is compared with world stocks (MSCI ACWI), with both measured in the same currency.

**Top and bottom.** Poland (88), the UK (82), Switzerland (82), Norway (81) and Canada (79) score highest. Poland fell 1.7% in pounds this week, one of the few markets to fall. The UK rose 3.4%. Spain (78) slipped from the top band to the second. The lowest scores are Argentina (6), Indonesia (6), Turkey (9), China (13) and Chile (17), all in "Avoid" and in a downtrend. Chile dropped into "Avoid" this week; the Philippines (21) moved out of it.

**Brazil.** Brazil had the best week of any country: up 12.6% in pounds. Its index rose 6.1% in reais, and the real rose 6.8% against the dollar. Even so, its score fell 5, to 36, and it dropped to the fourth band. The score looks at many months, and Brazil is still in a downtrend.

**Scores rising.** Denmark rose 10, to 69, into the second band. Sweden rose 8, to 74, the United States 7, to 73, Japan 6, to 67, and Australia 5, to 69.

**Scores falling.** New Zealand fell 11, to 47. Thailand (down 8) and Mexico (down 7) both dropped to the middle band at 59. Vietnam fell 8, to 23.

**Currencies.** Against the dollar, the Chilean peso fell 3.8%, the Mexican peso 2.7% and the Argentine peso 2.5%. Mexico's index rose 4.4% in pesos but only 2.1% in pounds.

**Cheap and holding up.** Mexico, Sweden, Norway, Poland and Hong Kong look cheap compared with other markets while keeping middling or better scores.

## Practice portfolios

The practice portfolios started on Tuesday 29 September with a pretend £10,000 each, one holding the top-scored 20% and one the lowest-scored 20%. All are still below their starting value.

- **Sectors:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £9,454. That is behind world stocks (£9,539), the picks' own markets (£9,658) and the lowest-scored portfolio (£9,931). US consumer staples (up 6.7% since the start) and biotech (up 4.7%) have done best among the top picks. The energy funds have done worst, with oil and gas exploration down 9.2%.
- **Countries:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £8,856, behind the lowest-scored (£9,332) and world stocks (£9,539). Taiwan, down 9.7% since the start, has cost the most. Indonesia, up 14.2%, has lifted the lowest-scored portfolio.

So far the lowest-scored picks are ahead in both. A couple of months of results mean very little. On past data, the top-scored 20% of sectors did 6.0% a year better than the lowest-scored 20%, with a reliability measure (t) of 1.3, where 2 or more is convincing. For countries the test gave −1.4% a year (t = −0.3): the score has not reliably picked winning countries.
