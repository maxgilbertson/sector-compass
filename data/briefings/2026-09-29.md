# Weekly briefing: week to Tuesday 29 September 2026

*This is the first edition. From now on the briefing covers Monday to Friday and arrives on Saturday mornings. This one covers the five trading days to Tuesday afternoon (UK time), so US and European prices were taken part-way through the day.*

## In brief

- It was a slightly negative week. World stocks fell 0.5% in pounds (1.6% in US dollars). The pound fell 1.1% against the dollar, which softened the losses for UK-based investors.
- Gold-miner funds had the worst week of any sector: down 8.5% and 8.1% in pounds. The global gold-miner fund's score fell 20, to 46 out of 100.
- Banks in Japan and Europe, global technology, chip makers and biotech still have the highest sector scores (83 to 88).
- Among countries, Poland (93), Taiwan (90) and Singapore (86) lead. South Africa and Argentina had the biggest score falls after losing more than 5% in pounds.
- The practice portfolios started today with £10,000 each. The first monthly re-pick is on Thursday 1 October.

## The big picture

World stocks (MSCI ACWI) fell 0.5% in pounds and 1.6% in US dollars; the gap is the pound's 1.1% fall against the dollar. The US market (S&P 500) fell 1.0% in dollars but rose 0.1% in pounds. Over the past year, world stocks are up 19.0% in pounds.

Our economic read is still **mid cycle**, the stage when the economy is growing steadily. The gap between the 10-year and 3-month US interest rates has widened by 0.47% in 3 months, which often happens when growth is picking up. The fear gauge (VIX, which measures how much investors expect prices to swing) is a calm 15.9, and the S&P 500 is still in its long-term uptrend. In this phase, technology, communication services, industrials and financials usually do well.

For countries, three conditions matter right now. Oil is up 31% over 3 months, which helps oil producers and costs oil importers. Investors are confident, which usually suits emerging markets. And chip makers did 8.4% worse than world stocks over 3 months, a drag on tech-heavy markets such as Taiwan and South Korea.

## Sectors

Each sector is compared with the whole stock market of its own region; for example, US technology is compared with the S&P 500. Scores run from 0 to 100 in five bands, from "Avoid" (below 20) to the top band (80 and above).

**Still on top.** The highest scores are banks in Japan (88, compared with the TOPIX) and Europe (85, compared with the STOXX Europe 600), global technology (84, compared with world stocks), and chip makers and biotech (83 each, compared with the S&P 500). Chip makers are up 92.3% in pounds over the past year. Three of these five are flagged "far above trend", meaning their prices are well above their long-term average. Biotech's score fell 8 after the fund dropped 3.1% in pounds.

**At the bottom.** China internet (6), US homebuilders (9), uranium and nuclear (10), US utilities (12) and Japanese real estate (13) are all in the "Avoid" band, and all are in a downtrend. China internet is down 36.4% in pounds over the year.

**Biggest moves.** Gold miners were the weakest group. The Canadian gold-miner fund fell 8.5% and the global one 8.1% in pounds, and the global fund's score fell from the second band to the middle one. Gold-miner shares tend to follow the gold price, with bigger swings. At the other end, Japan's electric appliances and precision fund rose 5.1% and its score rose 9, to 76. European technology rose 3.7% and is near a one-year high.

**Band changes.** Sixteen sector funds changed band, eight up and eight down. Canadian energy moved into the top band (81), and European car makers dropped into "Avoid". Canada had four moves up, including technology and consumer staples. Materials and metals-related funds moved down: global gold miners, global materials, and lithium and batteries. The full list is on the Sectors page under "What changed recently".

## Countries

Each country's main stock index is compared with world stocks (MSCI ACWI), with both measured in the same currency.

**Top and bottom.** Poland (93), Taiwan (90) and Singapore (86) score highest, followed by South Korea (79) and Spain (78). Taiwan is up 92.0% in pounds over the year and South Korea 144.3%. Both are "far above trend", and South Korea slipped out of the top band. The lowest scores are Indonesia (8), China (11), the Philippines (13), India (16) and Vietnam (18), all in a downtrend.

**A hard week for Latin America and South Africa.** The worst weeks in pounds were Argentina (−5.8%), Turkey (−5.6%), South Africa (−5.3%), Brazil (−4.4%) and Chile (−3.9%). South Africa's score fell 14 and Argentina's fell 13. Both, with Chile and Mexico, moved down a band.

Mexico shows how much the currency can matter. Its index rose 1.7% in pesos, but the peso fell 4.2% against the US dollar, so in pounds the market lost 1.3%.

**Moving up.** The UK (FTSE 100, now 61) and Belgium (64) moved up a band, and Germany rose to the middle band (47). The UK is one of the markets helped by rising oil.

**Cheap and holding up.** Brazil, Sweden, Norway, Poland and Thailand look cheap on price-to-earnings (share price divided by a year's profits) and dividends, while keeping middling or better scores.

## Practice portfolios

Today we started two pretend £10,000 portfolios on each page: one holds the top-scored 20% and the other the lowest-scored 20%. They will show whether the score picks winners from now on. Each shows £9,985 after the assumed trading cost, against £10,000 in their markets and in world stocks.

- **Sectors, top (17 funds):** these include banks in Japan and Europe, technology (global, US and Europe), chip makers, biotech, cybersecurity and cloud computing.
- **Sectors, lowest (17):** these include China internet, homebuilders, uranium and nuclear, solar, clean energy and US utilities.
- **Countries, top:** Poland, Taiwan, Singapore, South Korea, Spain, Japan and Norway.
- **Countries, lowest:** Saudi Arabia, Turkey, Vietnam, India, the Philippines, China and Indonesia.

A few weeks of results mean very little. For context, the same method was tested on past data:

- **Sectors:** the top-scored 20% did 6.1% a year better than the lowest-scored 20%. The reliability measure (t) was 1.3, where 2 or more is convincing. After trading costs, the top group beat its markets by 0.9% a year.
- **Countries:** the same test gave −1.9% a year (t = −0.4). The score has not picked winning countries.
