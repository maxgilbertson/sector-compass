# Weekly briefing: week to Friday 20 November 2026

## In brief

- A sharp fall: world stocks lost 7.6% in pounds (8.7% in US dollars) and the US market lost 7.5% in pounds (8.6% in dollars).
- Our economic read moved to **contraction**. The fear gauge (VIX) is at 35.9, where anything over 25 counts as stressed.
- Miners and chip-makers were hit hardest: copper miners, solar, semiconductors and gold miners each fell between 15% and 18% in pounds.
- Taiwan fell 22.3% in pounds and its score dropped 18, to 54. India was the only country to rise, up 4.0% in pounds.
- Steadier sectors held up best. US consumer staples fell only 0.9% in pounds and moved into the top band.

## The big picture

World stocks (MSCI ACWI) fell 7.6% in pounds and 8.7% in dollars this week. They are still up 5.8% in pounds over the year. The US market (S&P 500) fell 7.5% in pounds and 8.6% in dollars.
Our economic read is **contraction**. The S&P 500 is now below its 200-day (about 10-month) average price, a broad downtrend. The fear gauge (VIX, which measures how much investors expect prices to swing) is at 35.9; over 25 means investors are stressed. The gap between the 10-year US interest rate (4.91%) and the 3-month rate (4.12%) has shrunk by 0.21% in 3 months, which often happens late in an expansion. In this phase, consumer staples, health care and utilities usually do well.

For countries, four conditions matter today: the US dollar has risen 4.9% against major currencies over 3 months; copper is down 17% over 3 months, bad for mining-heavy markets; investors are nervous; and chip-maker shares did 15.8% worse than world stocks over 3 months.

## Sectors

Each sector is compared with the whole stock market of its own region; for example, US health care is compared with the S&P 500. Scores run from 0 to 100 in five bands, from "Avoid" at the bottom to the top band.

**On top.** US health care (87, compared with the S&P 500) leads, followed by European insurance (86, compared with the STOXX Europe 600), Japanese financials other than banks (86, compared with the TOPIX), US consumer staples (85) and European chemicals (84). US consumer staples moved into the top band after falling just 0.9% in pounds (2.0% in dollars, against the S&P 500's 8.6%).

**At the bottom.** Uranium and nuclear (1), solar (2), China internet (6), lithium and batteries (6) and robotics and AI (9), all compared with the S&P 500, are in "Avoid" and in a downtrend.

**Miners and chip-makers.** The worst weeks, all compared with the S&P 500, were copper miners (down 17.7% in pounds), solar (17.5%), semiconductors (16.9%), gold miners (15.3%) and uranium and nuclear (14.9%). In Canada, materials fell 14.9% and gold miners 14.8% in pounds; both dropped into "Avoid" (18 and 19, compared with the S&P/TSX 60). Miners' shares tend to follow metal prices, with bigger swings.

**Steadier sectors rising.** Japanese pharmaceuticals gained 3.0% in pounds and its score rose 15, to 82, into the top band (compared with the TOPIX). Canadian utilities gained 1.9% and rose 17, to 70, into the second band. US utilities moved up from the fourth band to the middle one (43).

**Japan gives ground.** Japanese banks fell 9.1% in pounds and lost 14, to 65. Electric power and gas lost 12, to 50, and dropped to the middle band, as did trading companies, foods, and raw materials and chemicals.

## Countries

Each country's main stock index is compared with world stocks (MSCI ACWI), with both measured in the same currency.

**Top and bottom.** Poland (90), Norway (83), Switzerland (82), the UK (81) and Spain (80) score highest. Norway, Switzerland and the UK all moved into the top band this week. Switzerland fell 3.5% in pounds, less than half the fall in world stocks. The lowest scores are Indonesia (5), Argentina (6), Turkey (10), China (13) and the Philippines (19), all in "Avoid" and in a downtrend.

**Taiwan and South Korea.** Taiwan fell 22.3% in pounds (17.5% in Taiwan dollars), the worst week of any country, and dropped from the second band to the middle one. Three of today's four conditions count against it, though it is still up 25.8% over the year. South Korea fell 12.7% in pounds. The weakness in chip-maker shares counts against both.

**India up.** India rose 4.0% in pounds (2.8% in dollars), helped by a 1.7% rise in the rupee against the dollar, and its score rose 13, to 58. Japan moved into the second band (61) even though it fell 8.0% in pounds, while Italy (51) and Denmark (59) dropped to the middle band.

**Currencies.** Against the dollar, the Israeli shekel fell 4.9%, the Chilean peso 4.6%, the Brazilian real 4.1% and the South African rand 3.8%. Brazil's market fell only 2.9% in reais, but 10.4% in pounds. South Africa fell 6.9% in rand and 11.9% in pounds.

**Cheap and holding up.** Mexico, Sweden, Norway, Poland and Hong Kong look cheap compared with other markets while keeping middling or better scores.

## Practice portfolios

- **Sectors:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £9,123, ahead of world stocks (£9,045) but behind the picks' own markets (£9,242) and the lowest-scored portfolio (£9,429). US consumer staples and European insurance have done best among the top picks, Canadian energy worst.
- **Countries:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £8,388, behind the lowest-scored (£9,041) and world stocks (£9,045). Taiwan, down 22.0% since the start, is the main reason.

About seven weeks of results mean very little. On past data, the top-scored 20% of sectors did 6.0% a year better than the lowest-scored 20%, with a reliability measure (t) of 1.3, where 2 or more is convincing. For countries the test gave −1.4% a year (t = −0.3): the score has not reliably picked winning countries.
