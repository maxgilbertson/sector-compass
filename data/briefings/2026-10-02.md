# Weekly briefing: week to Friday 2 October 2026

## In brief

- World stocks fell 0.8% in pounds (0.6% in US dollars). Technology and chip makers rose; gold miners, European banks and most European markets fell.
- The global and Canadian gold-miner funds fell 5.7% and 6.0% in pounds, and their scores fell by about 20 each, into the middle band.
- Japan's score rose 9, to 81, moving it into the top band, and several Japanese sectors moved up a band, led by steel and nonferrous metals (up 6.4% in pounds).
- Brazil had the best week of any country (up 3.5% in pounds) and the biggest score rise (up 22, to 73). Belgium, Italy and Spain each fell around 5% in pounds, and Germany and the UK moved down a band.
- The top-scored sector portfolio is slightly ahead of its own markets but behind world stocks; both country portfolios are behind world stocks. After a few days, this means very little.

## The big picture

World stocks (MSCI ACWI) fell 0.8% in pounds and 0.6% in US dollars this week. The US market (S&P 500) did a little better, down 0.4% in pounds and 0.2% in dollars. Over the past year, world stocks are up 18.8% in pounds.

Our economic read is **early-to-mid cycle**: the economy looks to be growing, possibly picking up speed. The gap between the 10-year and 3-month US interest rates has widened by 0.49% in 3 months, which often happens when growth is picking up, and copper (which follows factory and building demand) has gained 6.1% on gold over 3 months. The fear gauge (VIX, which measures how much investors expect prices to swing) is a calm 15.3. In this phase, technology, financials, industrials and materials usually do well.

For countries, two conditions matter. Oil is up 33% over 3 months, which helps oil producers and costs oil importers, although it is still down 18% over 6 months. And investors are confident, which usually suits emerging markets.

## Sectors

Each sector is compared with the whole stock market of its own region; for example, US technology is compared with the S&P 500. Scores run from 0 to 100 in five bands, from "Avoid" at the bottom to the top band.

**Still on top.** Banks in Japan (88, compared with the TOPIX) lead, followed by chip makers (87, compared with the S&P 500), global technology (86, compared with world stocks), Canadian energy (86, compared with the S&P/TSX 60) and US technology (84, compared with the S&P 500). All five are flagged "far above trend", meaning their prices are well above their long-term average. Chip makers rose 3.7% in pounds this week and are up 92.5% over the year.

**At the bottom.** China internet (3), homebuilders (8), Japanese real estate (9), uranium and nuclear (9) and solar (12) are all in the "Avoid" band and in a downtrend.

**Gold miners and Canadian materials.** The global gold-miner fund fell 5.7% in pounds and its score fell 20, to 42. The Canadian one fell 6.0% and its score fell 19, to 52. Canadian materials fell 4.8% and lost 15 points, to 55. All three dropped from the second band to the middle one. Gold-miner shares tend to follow the gold price, with bigger swings.

**Japan and energy moving up.** Japanese steel and nonferrous metals had the best week of any sector, up 6.4% in pounds, and its score rose 13, to 70. Japanese machinery and raw materials also moved up a band. With oil up over 3 months, oil and gas exploration rose 12, to 74, and US energy rose 9, to 80.

**European banks and health care down.** European banks had the worst week of any sector, down 6.1% in pounds (4.9% in euros), and left the top band with a score of 78. Health care was weak in several regions: the global fund's score fell 9, to 64, and European health care and Japanese pharmaceuticals both moved down a band. Elsewhere, Canadian financials moved into the top band (81), and US and global consumer spending on non-essentials moved up out of "Avoid". The full list of band changes is on the Sectors page.

## Countries

Each country's main stock index is compared with world stocks (MSCI ACWI), with both measured in the same currency.

**Top and bottom.** Taiwan (90), Poland (88), South Korea (86), Singapore (85) and Japan (81) score highest; South Korea and Japan moved into the top band this week. Taiwan is up 92.6% in pounds over the year and South Korea 143.3%. Poland's score fell 6 after a 4.3% fall in pounds. The lowest scores are Indonesia (6), China (10), the Philippines (12), India (17) and Vietnam (18), all in the "Avoid" band and in a downtrend.

**Brazil up.** Brazil rose 3.5% in pounds (4.7% in its own currency) and its score jumped 22, to 73, moving it up a band. It is helped by both rising oil and confident investors. South Korea (up 2.3%), Taiwan (up 1.1%) and Japan (up 0.8%) were the only other markets to rise in pounds.

**Europe down.** Belgium and Italy both fell 5.2% in pounds and their scores fell by about 10. Spain fell 4.9%. Germany (39) moved down a band, and so did the UK (FTSE 100, now 57), which fell 2.6%. The euro fell 1.1% against the US dollar, adding to the losses in pounds. Turkey had the worst week, down 6.0%.

**Currencies.** Chile's market fell 5.2% in pounds, and more than half of that came from the peso, which fell 2.9% against the US dollar. Mexico's market fell only 0.7% in pesos, but the peso fell 2.2%, so in pounds it lost 3.3%.

**Cheap and holding up.** Brazil, Sweden, Norway, Poland and Thailand look cheap compared with other markets while keeping middling or better scores.

## Practice portfolios

The practice portfolios started on Tuesday 29 September with a pretend £10,000 each. One holds the top-scored 20% and the other the lowest-scored 20%.

- **Sectors:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £10,024, ahead of the lowest-scored (£9,914) and the same money in the picks' own markets (£10,010), but behind world stocks (£10,051). European technology and chip makers have done best (both up 2.3%), Japanese banks and other financials worst.
- **Countries:** the top-scored portfolio is worth £9,983 and the lowest-scored £9,857, both behind world stocks (£10,051). Taiwan and South Korea are each up 3.3%.

A few days of results mean very little. For context, the same method was tested on past data. For sectors, the top-scored 20% did 6.0% a year better than the lowest-scored 20%. The reliability measure (t) was 1.3, where 2 or more is convincing. For countries, the same test gave −1.5% a year (t = −0.3): the score has not picked winning countries.
